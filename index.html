AI Image Evaluation Prep

Five Core Evaluation Dimensions

Every image comparison in high-stakes human rating anchors on these five pillars.
1. Instruction Following Strict fidelity to all explicit counts, text strings, colors, styles, and negative constraints requested in the prompt.
2. Visual Quality Absence of rendering artifacts, blurry smudges, unnatural pixelation, awkward seams, or unintended distortion.
3. Naturalness Coherent real-world logic, anatomical plausibility, believable perspective, and physically accurate light direction.
4. Correctness Factual accuracy, verifiable world knowledge, correct numerical values in charts, and error-free orthography.
5. Overall Preference A holistic comparative judgment weighing the severity of each candidate’s flaws against user intent.

Four-Step Sequence for Comparing Images

Use this systematic mental flow during your live screen-share or assessment test.
1
Extract the must-haves from the request
Deconstruct the prompt into atomic requirements (primary subject, exact count, required text, color modifiers, reference preservation).
2
Inspect A then B in the same scan order
Establish a uniform visual scan: focal subject first, numeric counts second, typography third, lighting and background fourth.
3
Lead with the most decisive evidence
Open your verbal justification with the single highest-severity failure (e.g., misspelled headline, corrupted data, or inverted physics).
4
Explain why the difference matters, then give the choice
Connect the defect to user utility, acknowledge any minor imperfection in the winning image out loud, and state your final pick cleanly.

When a Detail is Unreadable

Never guess or speculate during an evaluation interview.

If text, small counts, or fine textures are blurry or cropped at standard viewing size, do not guess and do not penalize an image for an error you cannot verify. Use this exact verbal script:

Verbatim Spoken Script
“Could you please zoom in on or enlarge this section of Image [A/B]? I want to verify the exact text spelling and count before making my evaluation, because I never want to penalize an image for an artifact I cannot clearly confirm.”
Golden Rule for Live Work
Live client and project instructions always override practice exercises. If an actual project rubric specifies that aesthetics outweigh minor typographic slips, or vice versa, follow that client’s rubric without hesitation.

Likely Interview Questions

Detailed breakdowns of common screening and calibration questions.

Evaluators are hired for precision, intellectual honesty, and calibration consistency. Expand each question to review the rubric requirements.

1. “Tell me about yourself and your background”
Reminder: Use only true personal details from your authentic background—never fabricate projects or employer names.
What a strong answer contains

A concise (under 90 seconds) summary connecting your analytical habits, visual attention to detail, or previous technical/academic writing to systematic evaluation. Emphasize comfort with strict guidelines and high-throughput rubric compliance.

Recommended Outline
  • Your educational or professional discipline (e.g., design, linguistics, research, software testing).
  • How you cultivated an eye for subtle anomalies, spatial consistency, or text verification.
  • Why structured, rubric-driven evaluation excites you over subjective reviewing.
2. “How do you maintain high quality and accuracy over long shifts?”
Reminder: Share your actual physical and mental stamina strategies—interviewers can spot unrealistic claims like “I never get tired”.
What a strong answer contains

Concrete operational systems rather than sheer willpower: standardized visual scan orders, checklist checklists before submitting, scheduled eye-rest micro-breaks, and dual-monitor or high-zoom cross-referencing.

Recommended Outline
  • Standardized scan protocol: Always verifying prompt must-haves in identical sequence so nothing slips through.
  • Active verification: Hovering or zooming into fine text and object counts rather than scanning passively.
  • Pacing discipline: Taking short 60-second micro-pauses every 45 minutes to prevent visual fatigue and rater drift.
3. “Tell me about a subtle error or mistake you caught”
Reminder: Draw from a real experience in proofreading, coding, grading, design, or data entry.
What a strong answer contains

A concrete situation where a superficial glance looked completely fine, but your methodical inspection revealed a critical underlying flaw (such as an inverted shadow, a swapped chart number, or a single missing character in a string).

Recommended Outline
  • Context: What project you were working on and why it appeared correct at first glance.
  • Discovery: The specific verification technique that caught the discrepancy.
  • Impact: How catching it prevented erroneous data, customer confusion, or downstream model degradation.
4. “What do you do when guidelines are ambiguous or silent on an edge case?”
Reminder: Never say you just “make a gut call and move on”. Calibration depends on team consistency.
What a strong answer contains

A three-tier protocol: consult existing project documentation/FAQs, check precedent in calibration channels, and if still unresolved, document your rationale explicitly while raising a targeted query to team leads.

Recommended Outline
  • Check project rubric precedents, benchmark sets, and Slack/Discord calibration logs.
  • Apply core first principles (instruction following and user utility) to break ties temporarily.
  • Log the edge case with image IDs and request an official ruling from project leads to update shared guidelines.
5. “What is your experience with generative AI and image models?”
Reminder: Be transparent about your exact level of tool usage (Midjourney, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, or text LLMs).
What a strong answer contains

Familiarity with common failure modes of diffusion and multimodal models: typography degradation, extra/fused digits, shadow incongruities, attribute bleed, and spatial reasoning lapses.

Recommended Outline
  • Hands-on familiarity generating images or evaluating output variations.
  • Understanding common model vulnerabilities: text rendering, object counting, relational prepositions (e.g., “on top of” vs “next to”).
  • Appreciation for the human-in-the-loop role in post-training alignment (RLHF/DPO).
6. “How do you handle calibration disagreements or auditor feedback?”
Reminder: Egos ruin annotation pipelines; high-performing evaluators welcome calibration audits.
What a strong answer contains

Detached professionalism. Focus on the written guideline text rather than personal opinions. Be willing to adjust your mental model immediately once a lead clarifies the guideline intent.

Recommended Outline
  • Separate personal aesthetic taste from strict rubric definitions.
  • Revisit the exact phrasing in the project guideline document together with the auditor.
  • Adopt the consensus standard to ensure future ratings match the benchmark distribution.
7. “What are your available hours and weekly commitment?”
Reminder: Over-promising hours leads to swift removal from active queues. State your realistic schedule.
What a strong answer contains

A crisp statement of your weekly bandwidth (e.g., 20 hours/week, 40 hours/week), your primary timezone, and your preferred uninterrupted focus windows.

Recommended Outline
  • Specific weekly hours you can guarantee consistently without schedule conflicts.
  • Your local timezone and typical working block (mornings, evenings, weekends).
  • Flexibility to attend occasional calibration syncs or complete urgent queue surges.

Thoughtful Questions to Ask the Interviewer

Asking sharp operational questions signals seniority and professionalism.
  • “How frequently are project rubrics and edge-case documentation updated as new model behaviors emerge?”
  • “What is the dispute or calibration workflow when two raters have diverging interpretations of an ambiguous prompt?”
  • “Is rater accuracy measured primarily through gold-standard test sets, consensus overlap, or lead auditor spot-checks?”
  • “What tooling is provided in the evaluation portal for pixel-level zooming and side-by-side element isolation?”
Strict Requirement: Eligibility & Location Truthfulness
Always answer questions regarding your physical location, tax residency, and legal work authorization with complete honesty and precision. Major AI lab vendors utilize geo-verification and strict compliance tooling; any attempt to misrepresent location, VPN status, or identity results in permanent platform blacklisting.
